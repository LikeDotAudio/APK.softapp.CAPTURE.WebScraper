## CardioidSE Electronics Gemini II

## Cardioid Tube Condenser Microphone

The Gemini II is the second edition of sE’s popular [Gemini dual-tube microphone](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/SE-Electronics/Gemini). Like the original, it uses a second dual-triode tube on the output circuit, where most mics would use a transformer or a transistor-based “transformerless” circuit.

[![Gemini microphone tubes](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/se/gemini-II-tubes-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/se/gemini-II-tubes.jpg)The differences between the original and the II include:

* The II has an onboard switchable -10dB pad.
* The II has a switchable high-pass filter (@ 100Hz).
* The shockmount has been improved.
* The power supply has been upgraded.
* Noise reduced from 16dBA (Gemini) to 12dBA (Gemini II).

The Gemini II is no smaller than the original — at 80mm in diameter and 230mm tall, it’s among the largest mics we’ve seen. Its 1200g (2.6 lbs) weight will challenge inferior mic stands.

[![sE Gemini II amplifier circuit](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/se/Gemini-II-circuit-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//mic_extras/se/Gemini-II-circuit.jpg)As in the Gemini, the two tubes in the II are a 12AX7/ECC83 on the input stage (capsule preamplification) and a 12AU7/ECC82 on the output (impedance conversion). Both are dual-triode tubes and are installed in sockets, easing repair/upgrades. Because the second tube is handling impedance conversion, there is no transformer in the circuit.

The amplifier circuit is built on a pair of densely-populated PCBs, using through-hole components. Switches on the mic body provide access to the aforementioned -10dB pad and high-pass filter.

[![Gemini II Flight Case](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/se/gemini-II-kit-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//mic_extras/se/gemini-II-kit.jpg)The mic ships with a wooden storage case, a shockmount, the dedicated power supply, a power cable, and an aluminum flight case.
