## CardioidMicrotech Gefell M 1030

## Cardioid Condenser Microphone

The M 1030 is a large-diaphragm FET condenser microphone with a fixed cardioid pattern. It is similar in components and design to the M 930, but offers a different sonic color.

As with the M 930, the M 1030 has a single-diaphragm capsule. It is similar to the M930 capsule, but is said by Gefell to deliver a more neutral sound.

[![M930 vs M1030 frequency-response comparison](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/graphs/gefell/M930-M1030-freq-sm.png)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//graphs/gefell/M930-M1030-freq.png)The published on-axis frequency-response graphs of the two mics are identical below 5000Hz. Between 7-15kHz, the M930 provides a smoother presence peak.

Note the off-axis response; the M930 provides up to 8dB more attenuation at 180° between 2–5kHz.

> Microtech-Gefell
>
> The advanced circuit design includes optical isolation of phantom power to deliver an ultra-clean target voltage to the capsule that gives an extremely low noise floor and high dynamic range.

The M 1030 capsule is shockmounted within the headbasket via an elastic suspension, providing isolation from structure-borne noise.

A green LED inside the headbasket indicates the microphone’s front, and lights to indicate that the amplifier and capsule polarization voltage are ready for use.  
[![M1030 in shockmount](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/gefell/M1030-shockmount-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//mic_extras/gefell/M1030-shockmount.jpg)

> Microtech-Gefell
>
> In comparison to the M930 it sounds more present in the mid range.

The mic ships with a mic clip (MTG p/n MH 93.1) in a wooden box. An elastic shockmount (p/n EA 92 with adapter A 93) is available separately.

> Sound On Sound, 2011
>
> I compared the M1030 against a [Neumann TLM103](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Neumann/TLM103) and… found it extremely hard to separate the two for on-axis sources…
>
> When tried on a variety of instruments the M1030 performed extremely well, always delivering a very usable and detailed sound. It could not be described as a clinical microphone, and it has a nicely full-bodied and slightly warm sound quality, but this doesn’t overwhelm the source character at all — and it’s beautifully balanced with the ‘bite’ of the presence peak.

The [Microtech Gefell M 1030](//recordinghacks.com/microphones/Microtech-Gefell/M-1030) is also known as: M1030.

The mic was released in 2010.
