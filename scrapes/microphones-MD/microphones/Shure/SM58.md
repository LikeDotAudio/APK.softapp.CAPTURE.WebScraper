## Cardioid Dynamic Microphone

Perhaps the most widely known vocal mic in the world, the SM58 celebrated its 40th anniversary in 2006.

It is a cardioid dynamic mic, designed around the same Unidyne III capsule as the SM57. In fact, the SM58 differs from the [SM57](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Shure/SM57) in only a few respects:

* The ball grille contains a foam blast filter.
* The ball grille reduces the bass boost due to proximity effect, simply because it prevents the mic’s capsule from being positioned so close to a sound source.
* The dynamic cartridge has a different resonator disk than is found on the SM57 (although the cartridges are internally identical).

![Shure R59 capsule for SM58](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/shure/r59_capsule.jpg)The mic can be used with the ball grille removed, but beware that the diaphragm would then be exposed, and therefore easily damaged. Replacement capsules (p/n R59; street price ~$70) can be obtained from Shure, and easily installed by soldering two or three wires.

More features:

* Frequency response tailored for vocals, with brightened midrange and bass rolloff
* Uniform cardioid pickup pattern isolates the main sound source and minimizes background noise
* Pneumatic shock-mount system cuts down handling noise
* Effective, built-in spherical wind and pop filter

The mic’s “pnuematic shock-mount system” insulates the cartridge from mechanical vibration in a way that is superior to typical mechanical isolation mounts. The result is that Shure’s SM-series dynamic microphones have lower handling noise than competitive models.

![SM58 Torture Test](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/shure/sm58_torture1.png)Check out the [SM58 torture test](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33QPLbQi9FI) — a YouTube video in which a new SM58 is hammered, dropped, dunked, frozen, and driven over — twice — and continues to work. (More or less.) In Part II, the mic is buried for a year.

*How is the SM58 different from the [Beta 58](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Shure/Beta-58A)?*

* The Beta 58 has a supercardioid pickup pattern.
* The Beta 58 has a 4dB hotter output than the SM58.
* The Beta 58 has less handling noise than the SM58.
* The Beta 58 has a hardened grill that is very difficult to dent.
* The Beta 58 has extended low end and high end compared to SM58.

… in other words, it’s a completely different mic, and it sounds different.

The SM58, like the SM57, can be modified in at least two ways:

1. The transformer can be removed; see details on the [SM57](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Shure/SM57) page.
2. The stock transformer can be swapped for a unit from TAB-Funkenwerks, giving the SM58 a wider frequency response and a sound similar to the [SM7](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Shure/SM7B); see [TAB-Funkenwerks transformer upgrades for the SM58](http://www.mercenary.com/tab58.html).

The SM58S incorporates an on/off switch.

The [Shure SM58](//recordinghacks.com/microphones/Shure/SM58) is also known as: SM-58, SM58.

The mic was released in 1966.
