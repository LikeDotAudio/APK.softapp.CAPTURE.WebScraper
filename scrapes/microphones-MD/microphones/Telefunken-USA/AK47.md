## Multi-Pattern Tube Condenser Microphone

The AK47 is a large-diaphragm multipattern tube condenser microphone.

![M16 Mk. II vs AK47 frequency response](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/graphs/telefunken-usa/M16-vs-AK47-frequency.jpg)Although based on the same K67-style capsule as the company’s M16, the AK47 has a much different tone or color due to the differences in circuit design, tube model, and transformer.

During its conception, the AK47 was intended to be a [limited-edition “signature” mic](http://www.gearslutz.com/board/1537673-post111.html), named for Alicia Keys. The signature line never materialized, but the name stuck.

Both mics are part of Telefunken Elektroakustik’s “RFT” series (for *Radio Fernseh Technik*). This line of microphones combines imported parts with selected Western components with the intention of creating professional microphones at prices far below what the company charges for its vintage reproductions.

[![Telefunken Elektroakustik AK47 Circuit](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/telefunkenusa/AK47-circuit-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//mic_extras/telefunkenusa/AK47-circuit.jpg)The AK47 amplifier circuit is new, not a vintage reproduction. It was [designed](http://www.tab-funkenwerk.com/id35.html) by Oliver Archut at AMI/TAB Funkenwerk, the supplier of the mic’s BV47 output transformer. The vacuum tube is a NOS Telefunken EF-732 sub-miniature tube.

The capsule is a K67 derivative, despite the “47” in the microphone’s name. Telefunken’s internal part number is TK47, but regardless it is the same China-made K67 copy as found in the M16. It is a center-terminated, large-diaphragm design with dual 6-micron membranes.

A switch on the power supply allows selection of omni, cardioid, figure-of-8, or intermediate patterns (9 total).

[![Telefunken Elektroakustik AK47](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/telefunkenusa/AK47-kit-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//mic_extras/telefunkenusa/AK47-kit.jpg)The AK47 ships with an elastic shockmount, 7-pin cable, PSU, and a padded wooden box for the mic.

> Electronic Musician, 2006
>
> For general use I prefer a large-diaphragm condenser with more midrange and low-end warmth [[than the M16 Mk II](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Telefunken-USA/M16)], and that’s just what the AK 47 delivers. I was consistently impressed by this mic’s rich tone, graced by an extended high end that kept it from being too warm or undefined.

> SoundOnSound, 2008
>
> With the AK47 I got a great sound both really close to the singer and 12-14 inches away. In fact, it seemed that wherever I pointed it I got a positive result, and I needed little or no EQ to arrive at the sound I wanted.

The [Telefunken Elektroakustik R-F-T AK47](//recordinghacks.com/microphones/Telefunken-USA/AK47) is also known as: AK-47.

The mic was released in 2005.
