## Multi-Pattern Tube Condenser Microphone

The Telefunken Elektroakustik Ela M 251 is a three-pattern tube condenser microphone, presented as a historically-accurate recreation of the AKG-built vintage mic of the same name.

Like the original ElaM 250/251, the Telefunken Elektroakustik recreations use a Telefunken AC701 vacuum tube in the amplifier circuit. (The vintage ElaM 250E and 251E, intended for export, used a 6072a tube.)

[![Telefunken Elektroakustik Ela M 251 Kit](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/telefunkenusa/ElaM-251-mount-sm.jpg)](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images//mic_extras/telefunkenusa/ElaM-251-mount.jpg)The capsule is a recreation of the [AKG CK-12](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/tag/ck12), arguably one of the most complex condenser capsules in the world. It is a large-diaphragm, edge-terminated design with gold-plated Mylar diaphragms. Telefunken Elektroakustik has invested considerably into the research, tooling, and process development necessary to recreate these capsules; see additional notes on the profile for the [ElaM 251 E](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Telefunken-USA/ElaM-251E).

Pattern selection is effected via a switch below the headbasket. The ElaM 251 provides Cardioid, Omni, and Figure-of-8 polar patterns.

The output transformer is built by Haufe, provider of the original 14:1 transformer to AKG for the Telefunken ElaM microphones, although there is some dispute about the authenticity of the construction.

> Klaus Heyne
>
> Transformers used in clones are not the same as in the original ELA M 250/251 design… Simple DC resistance tests alone will show that the current generation of these transformers has grossly different winding ratios. If you then take the transformer apart, you will also notice that it was wound to a different pattern than the original — all this adds up to a noticeably different sound…
