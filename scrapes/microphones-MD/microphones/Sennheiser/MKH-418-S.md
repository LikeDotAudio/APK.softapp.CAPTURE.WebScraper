## Multi-Pattern Condenser Stereo Microphone

The Sennheiser MKH 418 S is a Mid-Side Stereo shotgun microphone. At 280mm, it can be considered a medium length shotgun.

Two condenser capsules are used in the 418 S. The Mid capsule is similar to the MKH 416, which has a supercardioid pickup at low frequencies and narrow lobar pickup above 4kHz.

The Side signal is a bidirectional capsule with the positive side directed to the left of the microphone.

Both capsules use Sennheiser’s [RF Condenser](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/tag/rf-condenser) technology, which uses a relatively loose diaphragm tension and low polarization voltage. Benefits include low self-noise and better performance in changing weather conditions.

“TOP” is labelled on the microphone to aid in correct positioning.

The microphone has a 5-pin XLR jack, which provides the separate Mid and Side output channels. The microphone *does not* decode the MS signal to XY; the user would have to construct a mid-side matrix in an analog console or DAW to produce a LR stereo signal. (Read more about [mid-side](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/tag/mid-side), or watch a [mid-side demo video](http://recordinghacks.com/2010/11/14/mid-side-technique-mk012-km88/).)

Oddly, the necessary Y cable to separate the mic’s two output channels does not appear to be an included accessory (although it is available separately; see p/n Y-Cable AC 20 XLR-5F / 2 x XLR-3M).

See also the Sennheiser MZAMS-1, an external power supply and mid-side decoder. It provides a 5-pin XLRF input, a line/mic output level switch, a high-pass filter, a phase-reverse switch for the “side” channel, and adjustable level/width controls (which affect the relative gain levels of M and S channels). Presumably this box has dual XLR3M output jacks.

The [Sennheiser Electronics Corporation MKH 418-S](//recordinghacks.com/microphones/Sennheiser/MKH-418-S) is also known as: MKH418.
