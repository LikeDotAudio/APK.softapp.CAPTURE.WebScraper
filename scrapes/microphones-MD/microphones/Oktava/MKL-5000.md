## Multi-Pattern Tube Condenser Microphone

The MKL-5000 is a multipattern, large-diaphragm tube mic in the classic “bottle” format.

The capsule is a 34mm design, with twin 5-micron, 27mm diaphragms. The capsule is shockmounted within the headbasket assembly, which measures a large 64mm in diameter.

[![MKL5000 Circuit Photo, by Saturn Sound](//cdn.recordinghacks.com/images/mic_extras/oktava/mkl5000-circuit-sm.jpg)](http://www.saturn-sound.com/images/oktava%20mkl-5000%20-%20Inside.jpg)The microphone’s amplifier is a Class A tube circuit using a Russian 6Ж1П (EF95/6AK5) miniature pentode tube.

The tube power supply is described as “British designed,” but is manufactured in China, according to one of Oktava’s distributors.

A switch on the microphone body provides access to three polar patterns: cardioid, figure-8, omnidirectional.

The mic ships with a spider shockmount, tube power supply and power cable, in a padded metal flightcase.

> SoundOnSound, 2004
>
> The MKL5000 certainly boasts the usual valve qualities of a rich, full-bodied and musically flattering sound, although it’s clearly not in the same sonic league as, for example, the Neumann M149, the [Blue Bottle](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Blue-Microphones/Bottle), or the Brauner VM. Coming back to a more realistic UK price comparison, though, the most obvious competition comes from the impressive [Rode K2](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Rode/K2), and a decision really would come down to personal preferences. The K2 has technically superior specifications and a slightly smoother, more polished sound, but the MKL5000 looks a lot more impressive (to my eyes) and has a character that may well appeal to some over its antipodean counterpart.
