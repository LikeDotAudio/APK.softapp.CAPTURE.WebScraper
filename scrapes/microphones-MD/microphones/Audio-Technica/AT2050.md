## Multi-Pattern Condenser Microphone

The AT2050 is a medium-diaphragm multipattern FET condenser microphone with transformer-coupled output.

The capsule has two gold-sputtered diaphragms and is externally biased. Although described by A-T as a large-diaphragm design, the active diaphragm area measures just 20.4mm across. Backplate diameter is also shy of the traditional “large” cutoff, at 24.3mm.

The mic has unusually high SPL handling capabilities, spec’d at 149dB without the pad or 159dB with the pad engaged.

At 7.9mV/Pa, the AT2050 is much less sensitive than its single-pattern lookalike sibling, the AT2035 (22 mV/Pa), due to the need to accommodate the signal of the rear capsule in all modes but Cardioid.

A switch on the mic body allows selection of Cardioid, Omni, or Figure-of-8 pickup patterns.

The mic’s stated frequency range is 20-20,000Hz, but the frequency plots show a HF -3dB point closer to 16kHz.

The AT2050 ships with a shock mount (p/n AT8458) and a storage pouch.

> ProAudioReview
>
> There also seems to be quite a bit more “midrange punch” present in the AT2050 compared to the [4050](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Audio-Technica/AT4050) and [U 87](http://recordinghacks.com/microphones/Neumann/U-87-Ai), which I found to be helpful when putting the mic on a guitar cabinet. Those punchy qualities really helped me not need to reach for a midrange EQ boost in that upper-mid 3 kHz area.

The [Audio-Technica AT2050](//recordinghacks.com/microphones/Audio-Technica/AT2050) is also known as: AT-2050.

The mic was released in 2008.
